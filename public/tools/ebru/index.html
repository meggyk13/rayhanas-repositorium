About this tray

Ebru (the name is usually traced to Persian) was part of the Ottoman book arts by the 1500s: marbled sheets for album pages, borders and bindings, and sometimes as the ground under calligraphy. Battal, plain "stone" ebru made by sprinkling color after color, is the base every other pattern is built on. Combed forms like gelgit (back and forth) and tarak (comb) are drawn through a battal ground.

The physics is the mathematical marbling of Lu, Jaffer and colleagues: every drop, tine and swirl is an exact, area-preserving deformation, so colors never blend. They only stretch, as they do on sized water.1

Top bar. Undo and Redo step back and forward (Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z on a computer). Clear empties the tray to fresh water; you can undo it. Lift print makes a print.

Ink. Pick a pigment and tap the tray. Sprinkle the tray covers the whole tray in drops of that color; sprinkle three or four, largest size first, for your own battal. The palette button beside Size switches between Period (historical pigments), Modern (bright colors), Colorblind (colors that stay easy to tell apart, each named in the color sheet) and Your colors. + mixes your own: each one goes first in Your colors, which keeps your last 12; you can clear them from the palette list. Opacity sets how solid new ink is; lower lets the water, and the paper of a print, show through.

Settings (the gear). Sound: off, on, or on with gentle water sounds. Water sets how thin or thick the size is: thin water spreads drops wider and lets tools pull further. Tray color changes the water behind the ink: sea glass, pale, plain white, white with grey grid lines to help place your drops (the grid never shows on a print), or black. Calm water stills the moving light and ripples, and Tips on the tray hides the hints. Your name is filled in when you share, Paper sets the paper new prints start on, and Clear all removes the prints kept on this device. Auto mode has its own pace and timing.

Tools. Drop: tap, or hold to spread. Stylus: drag one needle. Rake: drag a row of tines, each drawing its own line; set how many beside Size. Swirl: hold to turn the water, clockwise or counter-clockwise (pick beside the ink). Mist: drag to spray fine drops. Rings: tap for concentric drops in your ink and a second color (pick it with the “with” button, or leave it on Auto), and set how many rings a tap lays (up to 24) beside Size; pull a stylus through the middle for a tulip. Drop, Mist and Rings lay ink and show its color; Stylus, Rake and Swirl only move the water. Size sets the scale of all of them.

Combs. Pick a direction, set the tine spacing and strength, then tap a comb to draw it through the whole tray.

Recipes. Choose a pattern, set the four layer colors (layer 1 is the largest drops; tap a color to change it, or Shuffle for another set), and tap Make it. It starts on a fresh tray.

Auto mode. Switch it on, confirm that the water can be cleared, and the tray marbles itself: it sprinkles, combs, lifts a print into Gallery › Yours for this visit, and starts again. Tap the tray or the switch to stop. Settings sets its pace and how long each print shows.

Printing. Lift print lays a sheet on the water and shows the print. Under Shape, layout & paper, choose a shape, a layout (full, album page with a ruled center panel, or mounted) and a paper color, then Download (Save or share on a phone), or press and hold the print to save it. Start over clears the tray; Keep editing goes back to it. Gallery › Yours keeps your newest 24 prints on this device, even after you close the page; open one to remove it.

Gallery. Add to gallery shares a print with everyone. You can give it a title and your name; both are optional. The newest 100 are kept, each with the date and time it was made. Gallery › Everyone’s shows the latest 24; the gallery page has them all. Open anyone’s print to cut it to another shape or layout and download it; the design and paper stay as its maker made them.
